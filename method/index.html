How it works

What happens every day between the slate opening and a pick landing on your phone.

Every morning at 7:54 a scheduled run prices the whole slate, researches the best spots, and posts a pick with two backups by 10:00. At 3:53 PM it rechecks the prices for that night. Every hour from 1 PM to 2 AM another run grades finished games, records the closing line, and updates this site.

1. Screen every game

The run prices every market it can find (moneylines, totals, spreads, team totals and first-five-innings bets) for every game in the sports the model covers, using quick team stats and odds from at least two sportsbooks. That's usually dozens of games and well over a hundred bets.

2. Research the best spots

The top eight or so from the screen get proper research: confirmed starters and goalies, injuries, fresh team numbers, more sportsbooks, and adjustments for news the numbers miss. Then they're priced again.

3. The market's view

Sportsbook odds include a cut, so the probabilities they imply add up to more than 100%. The model removes that cut from each book's prices (the "power" method, which takes a bit more off long shots) and takes the middle value across books. That's the market's fair probability.

4. The model's view

Each sport has its own score model that prices the exact bet, including pushes and overtime rules:

5. Blend and adjust

The market and the model each get half the say. Then up to four adjustments cover things the inputs can't see, like a tired bullpen or a late scratch. Each one is small, medium or large (about 1, 2 or 3 percentage points), and together they can't move the number more than about 5 points.

6. Pick and backups

  • Expected value: the final probability against the actual price. Positive means the price pays more than the risk deserves.
  • Ranking: the pick is the candidate with the best edge for its risk, so a solid edge at short odds beats the same edge on a long shot.
  • Size: a quarter of the Kelly stake, between 0.25 and 2 units. One unit is 1% of your bankroll.
  • Take at: the lowest price that still gives at least +1% expected value. Below that, skip the bet.
  • Confidence: High at +4% expected value or more, Medium at +2% to +4%, Low below that.

A stat model has to back the pick. Bets priced only from the odds can't show an edge (you'd just be paying the bookmaker's cut), so they rank last.

The next two best bets from different games become backups, each with its own "take at" price. If Stake's price on the main pick is too low, take backup 1, then backup 2. Only the main pick counts toward the record.

There's always a pick. If nothing has a positive edge, it's the closest call at the minimum size, marked "No clear edge".

7. Afternoon price check

Odds move during the day. At 3:53 PM the run gets fresh prices for the main pick and both backups, reprices them, and posts which one to bet, or to skip if none still has value.

8. Grading and the closing line

Once the game ends, the result is graded and the closing odds are recorded. Beating the closing price means the market moved toward the pick after it was made. Over many picks, that's the earliest reliable sign the edge is real, long before the win-loss record means much.
